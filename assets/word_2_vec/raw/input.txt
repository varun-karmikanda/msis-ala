At MIT Manipal, placements are currently in full swing.
 
Every day brings a different story.
 
Some students receive an offer.
Some make it through multiple rounds and miss out in the final one.
Some face rejection after preparing for weeks.
And then, surprisingly, they come back the next day and sit for another process.
 
That last part interests me the most.
 
Because placement is perhaps one of the earliest places in a student’s life where they experience rejection repeatedly and publicly while their peers are moving ahead.
 
And yet, the process continues.
 
A student who was rejected by Company A may get selected by Company B.
A student who cleared every technical round may not clear the HR round.
A student who thought, “This company is perfect for me,” may not get through.
 
Another student, who had almost given up, may suddenly receive an offer.
So, what exactly are we measuring when we say a student is “placement ready”?

Is it just CGPA?
Coding ability?
Communication?
Technical knowledge?
 
Or is it also the ability to absorb rejection, learn from it, recalibrate and walk back into the next interview with confidence?

Perhaps that is one of the most underrated employability skills.
Resilience.
 
As placement teams, we facilitate opportunities.
As educators, we prepare students for them.
But ultimately, students have to face the interview room themselves.
 
Our job is not to ensure that every student gets selected by the first company.
Our job is to ensure that every student keeps getting better after every attempt.
Placement season, therefore, is not merely a season of offers.
 
It is a season of growth.
 
And sometimes, the most important outcome of a rejection is not the rejection itself it is the student who walks back into the next process stronger than before.

To every student currently waiting for that “Congratulations” mail, Keep Going. Your placement journey is still being written.
